## Lagos

### A boy is born · *Nov 8, 1996*
On November 8, 1996, Mr and Mrs Ilozulu welcomed their first son, Ilozulu Christopher Chiduso. Known to family and friends as Chris, and to some others as .................. Our journey starts here.

### A full house
Two more boys and a girl would follow, making Chris the second of six. Growing up in the middle of a crowd teaches you to share, to stand your ground, and to look out for people. He'd need all three. And it all unfolded in one city.

### Stop one: Lagos
Nursery, primary, secondary. Chris's whole childhood happened here, and the boy who left was nothing like the boy who arrived.

### Lagos mornings ✎
Uniforms, traffic noise before sunrise, and football outside whenever the day allowed. Simple days, long afternoons.

### School days ✎
Classrooms, friends who knew him before anyone else did, and break-time snacks that were never enough.

### How many tubers?
His dad loved yam. Chris says that growing up, if you asked his dad for something like excursion money, he'd look at you and ask, "Do you know how many tubers I can buy with this?" Some arguments you just can't win.

## Owerri

### Stop two: Owerri
FUTO, the Federal University of Technology. This is where the cast grows.

### Roommates by chance · *Year 1*
I was randomly paired with Chris as a roommate in first year, and we quickly found we saw the world the same way. We were hooked on the Nigerian and diaspora startup scene (the early Paystack days, you remember the energy), and every semester break meant startup events in Lagos, together.

He found his footing in software engineering. I found mine in marketing. Twelve years on, it's still a "Count on Me" kind of friendship.

### Self-taught · *Year 3*
By year three, Chris was teaching himself to code on a small laptop running Kubuntu. I was hunting for a tech skill to learn when a mutual friend introduced us, and he pointed me to coding books and tutorials. (A story for another day.)

A shout-out to Kubuntu: may you stay a light load on the lives of humble, struggling students.

Year four passed in quiet hellos and one visit to his workplace during internship. Then final year came.

### The half-semester meal plan · *Year 5*
Final year hit me hard, and feeding became a struggle. Chris kept helping me out. For half a semester, he literally fed me, until things eased up. I'll be forever grateful. That's when we went from acquaintances to real friends.

### Of course he's Clean Code · *Year 5*
By year five, Chris had upgraded to a better laptop and was making real money from code. He was working for a company called Transhumanism, and he helped build STACC's election system *[confirm what STACC is]*. For a student, that was serious work. Somewhere in all of it, he also got close to his crush. (Another story for another day.). And so the nickname Clean Code was born. And why why wouldn't I call him that? Chris was balling.

He was still the only person I knew who wrote code, so the nickname was born: **Clean Code**. An inside joke, but why wouldn't I call him that? (FIFA still got its time. It still does.)

### The Harzard incident
Not every story was about code. One day, Chris and I went to the Owerri market to buy him shoes. On the way to the bus park, some boys said they could fix a pin on the shoe. We agreed, and that's how we met Harzard, who almost robbed us.

We got away. Egusi soup and a lot of thanksgiving followed. And then, almost before we knew it, graduation came.

### Paths diverge
Graduation came, and our paths diverged. Some of us headed to Abuja, others went their own ways, and Chris went back to Lagos. But this is not the end of the story.
